Redesigning Content Discovery Around User Behavior

Journey.do · Learning Management Platform

Users were struggling to find learning content despite dedicated navigation designed to help them do so. Research revealed that the problem wasn't simply content visibility. Users had developed a navigation model that differed from the one the product was designed around.

01 · Context

Supporting learning for system-involved youth

Journey.do is a learning management system designed to help youth strengthen protective factors associated with reducing further system involvement.

Youth are matched with evidence-based learning modules based on assessment results and input from supervising officers. Completing modules allows youth to earn certificates documenting their progress.

The youth-facing experience originally organized content across three primary areas: Grow for learning content, Connect for peer stories and reflections, and Feed for content already available to the user.

02 · Problem

Users couldn't find new content.

Officers repeatedly reported difficulty locating new content within the platform. When directed to the Grow page, some users did not recognize the feature despite its position in the primary navigation.

Rather than treating the issue as a training problem, I investigated how users were actually navigating Journey.do.

03 · Research

Understanding how users actually navigated

I interviewed probation and detention officers and administrators to understand where they experienced friction. During contextual walkthroughs, participants demonstrated how they normally navigated Journey.do and repeatedly referenced familiar platforms such as Facebook when describing how they expected the application to behave.

I then reviewed behavioral sessions in LogRocket. Users were relying heavily on the Feed to navigate through Journey.do while rarely attempting to use Grow as the primary content-discovery experience.

Because participants themselves referenced social platforms, I examined how familiar products structured feed-centered content discovery and used those patterns as comparative design research.

04 · Key Insight

Users weren't simply failing to find Grow. The product's navigation model did not match the mental model users brought to the experience.

The behavioral evidence and contextual walkthroughs suggested that users treated the Feed as a primary content-discovery surface rather than as a secondary destination.

Instead of adding more instructions directing users toward Grow, I proposed redesigning the product around the behavior users were already demonstrating.

05 · Design Direction

Make the Feed a first-class content discovery surface.

The redesigned Feed expanded the amount and type of information users could discover without requiring them to understand the platform's underlying content structure.

I designed a flexible card system for stories, protective-factor content, learning modules, and announcements. Cards provided more context before users opened them, including descriptions, story previews, and relevant imagery.

06 · Expanding Interaction

From content discovery to social interaction

After redesigning the Feed, I expanded its interaction model with messaging, reactions, comments, peer completion indicators, profile navigation, and contextual actions.

Feed-card context menus provided quick access to actions such as pinning, reporting, commenting, and navigating directly to the associated learning content.

07 · Implementation

Designing and shipping the experience

I was responsible for product planning, wireframing, front-end design, and front-end implementation. I translated the redesign into an epic, gathered user stories, and defined the development tasks required to implement the experience.

I collaborated with two senior backend engineers on the supporting backend architecture and independently connected and implemented the front-end experience.

08 · Outcome

Evaluating the redesign

Following implementation, I launched an NPS survey to evaluate the updated experience. NPS scores increased compared with the previous measurement.

The recurring complaints from users who could not locate content also stopped following the redesign.

09 · Reflection

Design around behavior, not assumptions.

The project changed how I approached usability problems. The original interface reflected how we expected users to navigate the platform, while behavioral research showed that users had developed a different mental model.

Rather than adding instructions to reinforce the intended flow, I redesigned the experience around navigation behavior users were already demonstrating.
